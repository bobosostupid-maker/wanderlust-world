Colosseum

History of the Colosseum

The Colosseum, also known as the Flavian Amphitheatre, is the largest amphitheatre ever built and one of the greatest works of Roman engineering. Located in the heart of Rome, it remains a powerful symbol of the Roman Empire.

Construction under the Flavians

Construction began around 70–72 AD under Emperor Vespasian. It was completed in 80 AD under his son Titus, and later modified under Domitian. The amphitheatre was built on the site of Nero’s private lake, returning the land to public use and winning popular support for the new dynasty.

Design and Capacity

The Colosseum could hold an estimated 50,000 to 80,000 spectators. A complex system of vaults, corridors, and staircases allowed crowds to enter and exit efficiently. Beneath the arena lay the hypogeum — an underground network housing animals, gladiators, and stage machinery.

Games and Spectacles

For centuries it hosted gladiatorial contests, animal hunts, public executions, and even mock naval battles in its early years. Events were free to the public and served as both entertainment and political propaganda.

Later Centuries

After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, the Colosseum suffered neglect, earthquakes, and stone-robbing. In the Middle Ages it was used as housing, workshops, and a fortress. Modern restoration and conservation have protected the monument. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980 as part of the Historic Centre of Rome.

Best Photo Spots

The exterior arches at golden hour, the view from Via dei Fori Imperiali, and upper-tier arena overlooks if your ticket allows.

✈️ Travel Tips for Visitors

  • Best time: Early morning or late afternoon. Spring and autumn offer the most pleasant weather.
  • Tickets: Buy online in advance. Combined tickets with the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill offer better value.
  • Getting there: Colosseo metro station (Line B) is right beside the monument.
  • Duration: Allow 1.5–3 hours; longer if you join a guided tour or visit underground areas.
  • What to bring: Comfortable shoes, water, sun protection, and a light layer for cooler months.
  • Local tip: A guided tour or audio guide makes the ruins far more meaningful than walking alone.
