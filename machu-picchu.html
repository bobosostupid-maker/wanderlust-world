Machu Picchu

History of Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is one of the most spectacular archaeological sites in the world. Nestled high in the Andes at about 2,430 metres above sea level, this 15th-century Inca citadel remains remarkably well preserved.

Built by the Inca Empire

Most historians believe Machu Picchu was built around 1450 during the reign of emperor Pachacuti Inca Yupanqui. It was likely a royal estate or sacred centre rather than a purely defensive fortress. The site contains about 200 structures, including temples, residences, storage buildings, and agricultural terraces.

Inca Engineering Marvel

The Incas were master stonemasons. Stones were cut and fitted so precisely that many walls still stand without mortar. The city shows advanced knowledge of astronomy, hydrology, and earthquake-resistant construction. Stone aqueducts channelled water, and terraces prevented landslides while maximising farmland.

Abandonment & Rediscovery

Machu Picchu was abandoned around the mid-16th century, near the time of the Spanish conquest. The conquistadors never found it. In 1911, American explorer Hiram Bingham, guided by local farmers, brought the site to international attention. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

Best Photo Spots

The classic Guardhouse viewpoint, the agricultural terraces at sunrise, and the Temple of the Sun area on clearer days.

Altitude Advice

Cusco sits at 3,400 m. Spend 1–2 days acclimatising before visiting Machu Picchu to reduce altitude sickness risk.

✈️ Travel Tips for Visitors

  • Best time: Dry season (May–September) for clearer views. June–August is busiest.
  • Tickets: Limited daily entries — book early and choose your circuit in advance.
  • Getting there: Fly or bus to Cusco, train to Aguas Calientes, then a short bus to the entrance.
  • Duration: A full day from Cusco is common; overnight in Aguas Calientes is more relaxed.
  • What to bring: Passport (required), hiking shoes, rain jacket, sunscreen, water, and snacks.
  • Local tip: The Inca Trail needs booking months ahead; shorter alternative treks are also excellent.
