Taj Mahal

History of the Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful buildings ever created and a supreme example of Mughal architecture. Located on the banks of the Yamuna River in Agra, it was built as a mausoleum and stands as an enduring symbol of love.

A Monument of Love

In 1631, Mumtaz Mahal, the beloved wife of Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan, died while giving birth to their 14th child. Overwhelmed by grief, Shah Jahan ordered the most magnificent tomb the world had seen. Construction began in 1632.

Design and Construction

The main architect is generally believed to have been Ustad Ahmad Lahori. The complex combines Persian, Islamic, and Indian traditions. The white marble mausoleum is flanked by a mosque and a guest house, with formal gardens in the classic charbagh pattern.

More than 20,000 artisans worked on the project. Materials came from across India and Central Asia — white marble from Makrana, jade and crystal from China, turquoise from Tibet, and lapis lazuli from Afghanistan. The main structure was completed around 1648; surrounding works finished by about 1653.

Later History & Heritage

After Shah Jahan was overthrown by his son Aurangzeb in 1658, he was imprisoned in Agra Fort, where he could see the Taj Mahal until his death in 1666. He was buried beside Mumtaz Mahal. The monument was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and remains one of the world’s most visited landmarks.

✈️ Travel Tips for Visitors

  • Best time: October to March for cooler weather. Sunrise visits are magical and less crowded.
  • Tickets: Buy online in advance. Foreign visitors pay a higher fee. Closed on Fridays.
  • Getting there: About 3–4 hours by express train from Delhi, or 2–3 hours by car.
  • Duration: Allow 2–3 hours for the complex, gardens, and museum.
  • What to bring: Modest clothing (shoulders and knees covered), comfortable shoes, water, and sun protection.
  • Local tip: Combine with Agra Fort the same day for a richer historical experience.
