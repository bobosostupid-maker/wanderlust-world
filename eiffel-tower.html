Eiffel Tower

History of the Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower is one of the most recognisable structures in the world and the ultimate symbol of Paris and France. Standing on the Champ de Mars, it was built as the centrepiece of the 1889 Exposition Universelle (World’s Fair), held to celebrate the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution.

The Competition & Design

In 1884, French engineer Gustave Eiffel and his company began developing plans for a tall iron tower. The design was mainly created by engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, with architectural improvements by Stephen Sauvestre. Eiffel’s proposal was selected from more than 100 competition entries for the 1889 World’s Fair monument.

Construction (1887–1889)

Construction started on 28 January 1887 and finished in just over two years. The tower was assembled from more than 18,000 iron pieces held together by 2.5 million rivets. At completion it was the tallest man-made structure in the world at 300 metres — a record it held until 1930.

Many Parisians initially disliked it. Artists and writers signed a petition calling it a “useless and monstrous” structure. Gustave Eiffel only had a 20-year permit; the tower was supposed to be dismantled afterward. Its value as a radio transmission tower saved it from destruction.

From Temporary to Permanent Icon

During World War I the tower intercepted enemy radio communications. Over the 20th century it became the beloved landmark we know today. Lighting systems were added over the decades, and every evening the tower sparkles with thousands of lights. Nearly 7 million visitors come each year.

✈️ Travel Tips for Visitors

  • Best time: Early morning or late evening for fewer crowds and beautiful lights. Avoid peak French school holidays if you can.
  • Tickets: Buy online from the official site in advance. Choose stairs or lift, and 2nd floor or summit access.
  • Getting there: Metro lines 6, 8 or 9 (Bir-Hakeim, École Militaire, Trocadéro). RER C also stops nearby.
  • Duration: Plan 1.5–3 hours depending on queues and whether you go to the top.
  • What to bring: Valid ID, comfortable shoes, and a light jacket — it can be windy at the summit.
  • Local tip: The free view from Trocadéro is excellent, especially when the tower sparkles on the hour after dark.
